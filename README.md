Study title: Long-term conspecific care of a disabled wild carrion crow (Corvus corone)


Abstract: 
Caregiving behavior towards the disabled has historically been considered a uniquely human trait, but its evolutionary origins have remained unclear. Although several cases of non-human animals exhibiting short-term caregiving behaviors towards injured, impaired, or imperiled conspecifics have been reported, long-term care like repeated food provisioning has been documented only rarely and, to our knowledge, exclusively in mammals. We describe here a disabled wild carrion crow (Corvus corone), approximately 12-18 months old and missing most of its upper beak, being regularly fed ("allofed") and preened ("allopreened") on typically self-accessible body parts by a pair of conspecifics. Allofeeds and allopreens occurred throughout the 6-month observation period, even while the disabled crow's solicitation behavior decreased over time. Despite being significantly less effective at feeding itself, the disabled crow successfully molted and has survived for nearly 16 months thus far. These findings challenge the view that long-term care behaviors are mammalian-specific, widening the taxonomic breadth of conspecific care and inviting a reassessment of theories surrounding the evolution of altruism and perhaps empathy in humans and non-human animals.

Folder/file overview:

GENERAL NOTE: In early stages of the project (data coding/processing/analysis), "disabled crow" was referred to as "broken beak" 
            and "BB" and the "healthy crows" were referred to as "other crows" and "control." These names turn up in the data files and code.

"Time Budget Files" folder: Time budget files for each video, exported from BORIS video coding software. Titles refer to the date, subject of the video (C = healthy crow, BB = disabled crow), and video number within a day.

"Disabled Crow Care Data Analysis and Plots.R": Full code for project (1) data importation and manipulation into a useable data frame, (2) models/analysis, (3) plots, and (4) main combined figure. Note that all minor errors in video coding were double checked within the video before being manually corrected in the data frame.

"Combined Beg Session Outcomes.csv": combined information on solicitation sessions and outcomes from video and field data (see methods). For import into "Disabled Crow Data Frames, Analysis, and Plots.R" as combined_beg_summary.

"Session Dates.csv": dates and lengths (seconds) of observation sessions. For import into "Disabled Crow Data Frames, Analysis, and Plots.R" as session_dates.
